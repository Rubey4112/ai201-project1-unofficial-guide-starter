**spinninspeakers**: Get an on campus job if you can during the spring semester. Use fall to make friends and get settled, but then find something to work at. I think having a job helped me manage my time better, and I met a tremendous amount of people. Faculty and staff as well as students recognize me because I worked all over campus as a student, and am employed here now full time.

That being said don't work too much. It is easy to get burned out. Study a little every day and you will be much better prepared come exams. Plus you can get a good nights sleep before tests instead of cramming.

If you do get a job, set a hard limit on how many hours your willing to work a week. Stick to it. If you do this you'll have plenty of time to study, hangout with friends, participate in extracurriculars, and make money.

Make friends with the RA. They are not the enemy. Most of them do all the shit you want to. Drinking, partying, and so on. They are usually decent people as long as you can respect that they have a job to do. Do your best never to put them in a position where they have to take punitive action.

I also recommend checking out fraternity and sorority life. Do your due diligence and decide if it's for you. I did it, and it was a very positive experience for me. Don't take all the Gossip and rumors at face value. Check it out and see if it's for you. It may not be and that's fine, but at least explore the options.

Do some sort of extracurricular. Debate or sports or cultural clubs. Get out there and meet people. If you can't find a club that interests you, then start one. Out of all the students here some are bound to share your interests.

Finally stay up late every now and then. Do something spontaneous and adventurous (I highly recommend a late night cheesesteak run to Philly). Get a good nights sleep most nights, but do crazy things every once in a while.

TL;DR: get involved at every opportunity!

**dssvo**: Apply for every scholarship you even have the slightest chance of getting.

Here's one way to think about it. Let's say you have a 5% chance of getting a scholarship you apply for. A 95% rejection rate. You apply for 20 scholarships, and get only 1 of them. Sound tedious? Suppose that the one scholarship you got pays for 1 semester of college, about $5000. Calculate that, it's a payout of $250 per essay you wrote. If each essay is 1000 words, you're making $0.25 **per word** even though 95% of what you're writing is being rejected. Some scholarships pay much more than that, and your chances of getting a scholarship are higher than you think.

Source: scholarships paid for my entire undergraduate degree.

About selecting classes: Challenge yourself to take as many hard classes as you can early on (like freshman and sophomore year) while you're still taking easy gen-eds. It's not fun to be in your senior year with all the hard 400-level classes all at the same time, try to spread it out so you don't die later.
